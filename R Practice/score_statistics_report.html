Mathematical Analysis of score_statistics.py

Extracted from the calculation pipeline in calculate_score_statistics — prepared for re-deduction in a manuscript. All formulas are LaTeX and rendered via MathJax.

1. Overview of the Algorithm

The function calculate_score_statistics processes a list of course records and produces four summary quantities:

  1. Total credit $C$
  2. Credit-weighted GPA $G$
  3. Credit-weighted average score $S$
  4. Incomplete count $I$ and course count $N$

The pipeline is: filter → validate credit → accumulate weighted sums → divide → handle empty cases.

2. Notation

Let the input be a collection of records. Only records that are mappings (dict-like) are kept; the rest are discarded entirely.

For each kept course $i$, define three raw fields:

  • $c_i$ — credit (coursePoint)
  • $g_i$ — GPA (gpa)
  • $s_i$ — score (score)

A value is called finite if it is a real number (not bool, not NaN, not $\pm\infty$), per the helper _finite_number. Denote the finite-validity predicate as:

$$ \text{finite}(x) = \begin{cases} \text{true}, & x \in \mathbb{R},\ x \notin \{\text{NaN}, \pm\infty\},\ x \notin \{\text{True}, \text{False}\} \\ \text{false}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$

3. Mathematical Formulation

3.1 Course Eligibility (Credit Gate)

A course $i$ is counted (enters courses and total_credit) if and only if its credit is finite and strictly positive:

$$ i \in \mathcal{C} \iff \text{finite}(c_i)\ \wedge\ c_i > 0 $$

If the credit is missing, non-finite, or $\le 0$, the course is excluded from all sums and contributes $+1$ to the incomplete count.

3.2 Total Credit

$$ C = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}} c_i $$

3.3 Credit-Weighted GPA

Let $\mathcal{C}_g = \{\, i \in \mathcal{C} : \text{finite}(g_i) \,\}$ be the subset of counted courses that have a valid GPA. Then:

$$ G = \frac{\displaystyle \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_g} g_i\, c_i}{\displaystyle \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_g} c_i} $$

with the convention:

$$ G = \text{None} \quad \text{if} \quad \mathcal{C}_g = \varnothing \quad \left(\text{i.e.}\ \sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_g} c_i = 0\right) $$

3.4 Credit-Weighted Average Score

Similarly, let $\mathcal{C}_s = \{\, i \in \mathcal{C} : \text{finite}(s_i) \,\}$. Then:

$$ S = \frac{\displaystyle \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_s} s_i\, c_i}{\displaystyle \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_s} c_i} $$

with:

$$ S = \text{None} \quad \text{if} \quad \mathcal{C}_s = \varnothing $$

3.5 Incomplete Count

The incomplete counter accumulates in two disjoint situations:

$$ I = \underbrace{\bigl|\, \{\, i \notin \mathcal{C} \,\} \,\bigr|}_{\text{invalid credit}} \;+\; \underbrace{\bigl|\, \{\, i \in \mathcal{C} : \neg\,\text{finite}(g_i)\ \vee\ \neg\,\text{finite}(s_i) \,\} \,\bigr|}_{\text{valid credit but missing GPA or score}} $$

Note: a course with valid credit but both GPA and score missing is still counted only once here (the if gpa is None or score is None is a single OR-check).

3.6 Course Count

$$ N = |\mathcal{C}| $$

3.7 Global Guard

The whole result is None if there is no usable data:

$$ \text{return None} \iff C \le 0 $$

5. Key Semantic Observations

  1. Asymmetric credit handling. A course with invalid credit is fully excluded (no GPA/score contribution, no credit). A course with valid credit but missing GPA/score still contributes its credit to $C$ and to the other weighted sum, but not to the missing one. So $C$ is not generally equal to $\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_g} c_i$ nor $\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_s} c_i$.
  2. Two different denominators. $G$ and $S$ are each normalized by their own credit sum, not by $C$. This means $G$ and $S$ are not weighted over the same population unless every counted course has both GPA and score.
  3. bool is rejected. Because bool is a subclass of int in Python, True/False would silently become $1.0$/$0.0$; the code explicitly blocks this.
  4. Non-finite values are treated as missing, not as errors — $\infty$/NaN never propagate into the sums.
  5. The None conventions (empty GPA set, empty score set, or $C \le 0$) are the "no data" sentinels.

6. Suggested Re-factoring Structure for the Manuscript

To re-derive this cleanly, present it as a two-stage estimator:

Stage A — Selection: define the eligibility sets $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathcal{C}_g$, $\mathcal{C}_s$ via the finite/positive predicates.

Stage B — Aggregation: define the weighted-mean operator

$$ \bar{x}_w(\mathcal{A}) = \frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}} x_i c_i}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}} c_i}, \qquad \bar{x}_w(\varnothing) = \text{None} $$

and then simply state:

$$ G = \bar{g}_w(\mathcal{C}_g), \qquad S = \bar{s}_w(\mathcal{C}_s) $$

This makes the code's logic a direct transcription of the math and much easier to verify.
